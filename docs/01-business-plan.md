# LastSeat India: Business Plan

> A subscription for last-minute empty seats, from about Rs 500 plus taxes.

## Origin of the idea
Idea from a creator video: seats on departing flights earn nothing once the plane leaves. Frontier Airlines' GoWild pass does this in the US; the idea is an Indian equivalent, e.g. an annual pass of Rs 25,000 for last-minute fares near Rs 500 plus taxes.

## Problem
Airlines fly with empty seats whose marginal revenue after departure is zero, while travellers who are flexible would take a trip if it were cheap enough.

## Solution
Flexible travellers buy an annual pass. Partner airlines release unsold seats close to departure; pass holders book them at a low fare plus taxes. Seats are explicitly unguaranteed: the airline can take them back, and affected members are refunded and given priority on the next flight.

## Target users
- Flexible travellers: freelancers, students, remote workers
- Frequent leisure flyers on busy domestic routes (BOM-DEL, BOM-GOI, BLR-GOI)
- Airlines with predictable unsold inventory on certain routes

## Revenue model
- Annual pass (core)
- Taxes/fees pass-through
- Airline revenue share or partner fee on seats sold
- Add-ons (baggage, hotel bundles)

## Key risks
- Airline willingness to partner (cannibalisation of full-fare sales is the key objection)
- Adverse selection: members only take trips that would have sold anyway; need fences (window, routes, caps)
- Customer anger when seats are withdrawn
- Dependence on airline inventory APIs
- Aviation regulator rules on fares and reselling tickets

## Success metrics
- Seats filled that would otherwise fly empty
- Cannibalisation rate vs full-fare sales (airline-measured)
- Pass renewal rate
- Bump rate
- Bookings per member per year
